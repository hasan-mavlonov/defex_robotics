## About this model.
×
What runs here
MuJoCo simulates only the plug and socket contact, in your browser. The complete cycle is a scripted illustration. Forces are examples and timing is not to scale.
How it places a connector
Two camera checks on every unit: the board, then the plug in the grip. The robot aims for the header and presses gently, so the chamfer guides the last fraction of a millimetre. A search is only a fallback: a fixed routine, not a trained robot policy.
How units flow
Each unit rides on a pallet: the board in its fixture, the harness housing in a nest, its far end in a test adapter. Loaded pallets come up the lift at the right. The robot works one pallet while the next waits behind it. At the left a picker packs each passed unit on edge into an ESD tote that holds four; a full tote rolls off to the tote store, then belts rise between the rollers and carry the empty one waiting in front across into its place. Empty pallets return under the bench. A reject stops the line until a person clears it; the robot then posts the rejected harness into the locked bin.
What it checks
The latch force signature, a pull, and continuity and isolation on every wire.
Not yet proven
False passes from half-seated plugs, damage at seating force, pallet repeatability, cycle time, and safety near people.
Next
Build the robot and run it on customer parts. Track grip and header offsets across units to flag a worn fixture or gripper early.

### Robot Learning Engineer — Manipulation
Full-time
San Francisco, CA
Teach the robot connector work from demonstrations and its own tries, and measure how well it holds up.
#### About the role
We want the robot to learn connector work from demonstrations, touch and its own test results. You’ll find out where learning helps and what it takes to switch to a new connector.
You’ll own data, training and running models on the robot, with the founders and the controls engineer. A result counts when the joint passes its test, the robot recovers on its own and no one has to step in. Start date follows the prototype build.
#### Pay
$150,000–$180,000 USD per year + equity.

Build tools that record demonstrations and corrections, in sync with what the robot did and whether the part passed.
Train imitation-learning policies. Try reinforcement learning where it fixes a real failure.
Run models on the robot within its calibration, latency and control limits.
Version data and checkpoints. Build tools to replay attempts and compare runs.
Test policies against hand-written control on fresh parts: misses, damage, manual fixes.
Move to a second connector and count what it took: demos, tooling and engineering time.

You’ve trained a policy, run it on a real robot and dug into why it failed.
Training and evaluation code in Python, with PyTorch or JAX.
Hands-on imitation or reinforcement learning, and how the data you collect shapes the result.
You know robot observations, actions and timing, and can debug everything around the model.
You can set up a test that tells a better model apart from a change in parts or fixtures.
