# heldout2_v0.2f report

(Written as REPORT.txt: writing REPORT.md was blocked.)

Seed 20261207. Controllers frozen at ddd0d34 (v0.2e). Suite code 6ccb8ea. Run from 6ea164e,
which adds only the pre-registration. 8,000 paired trials: ten controllers and ten
conditions (five families, two levels each), 40 trials per condition, on the gantry and the
UR5e arm. Run details are in RUN_LOG.md. Every number below comes from HELDOUT2_TABLES.md or
TABLES.md in this folder, or from */trials.csv where a line says so. Simulation only.

## Result in one paragraph

These families were new: nobody had developed against them. In 5 of 10 machine-family cells
C is ahead of every fair baseline (D, E, F, H, I). In 2 a fair baseline matches C, and in 3 C
is below the best fair baseline. C is ahead on unseen receptacle tilt and yaw on both
machines, and on the tight Micro-Fit part on the arm. C is below F on the unseen in-hand
offset of the Micro-Fit on both machines, and below D on F/T drift on the arm. Over each
machine's whole suite C is ahead (gantry 94.0% vs F 83.2%, arm 88.8% vs F 79.2%), but most of
that pooled lead comes from the yaw family. C passed no bad units on the gantry. On the arm C
passed 8, all at 0.3 N/s F/T drift. That is more than H (0) and I (1). These are C's first housing-only
bad units in a reported run since Checkpoint 2 (C passed 6 in benchmark_v0.2b and 29 in the
v0.2a heldout run; corrected after the report was written). G gave no measurable gain in
any cell. The pre-registered expectation ("matches in most cells") was wrong. Hasan decides
what this means for the pitch.

## Against expectation

The pre-registered expectations came from the published results only.

- Expected: matches in most cells. This was wrong: only 2 of 10 family cells match. In
  benchmark_v0.2e, C's lead sat in high camera error. Here the camera is nominal (sigma
  0.3 mm), yet C is still ahead on unseen socket rotation and on the tight Micro-Fit part on
  the arm.
- Expected: unseen tilt and yaw would behave like the hand-eye bias family (I 100 of 100).
  This was wrong: I seated 37.5% on unseen yaw and 85-91% on unseen tilt. A spiral search
  finds an unseen lateral error such as the hand-eye bias. It does not find unseen yaw or
  tilt: F's and I's plugs go to full depth still rotated (see the bad units above).
- Expected: C passes fewer bad units than H and I on the housing-only families. True on
  tilt and yaw. Not true on F/T drift: on the gantry no controller passed one, and on the arm
  C passed more.
- Expected: no bad-unit difference on Micro-Fit. True.
- The published pattern that C loses to I under an unseen lateral error held on the
  Micro-Fit unseen in-hand offset: C is below F on both machines.

## Post-hoc observations (decided after seeing the results; not pre-registered)

- Post hoc, unseen grasp:
  - C's Micro-Fit failures fall where the slip and the tilt have the same sign, so the
    tip offsets add (0.4 mm + 0.42 mm).
  - At 2 deg with the same sign, C seated 6 of 24 on each machine; F seated 23 and 21 of
    24. With opposite signs, C and F both seated 16 of 16 (trials.csv).
  - Of C's 36 failures at 2 deg, 35 end above the mouth (depth -2.3 to -4.5 mm). One, on the
    arm, ends at 7.4 mm.
  - The gantry failures stop at the retry limit after 7 retries; the arm failures stop at
    the time limit after 5-7 retries.
  - C sizes its search prior from the told accuracy (0.3 mm sigma), so a 0.8 mm offset sits
    at about 2.7 sigma. This is a reading of the design, not a traced cause.
- Post hoc, F/T drift:
  - C fails only at 0.3 N/s on the arm, where its cycles are longer: median 8.9 s over the
    40 trials, against 5.3 s on the gantry. 14 of C's 40 arm cycles there ran 15.8-27.4 s.
  - The false accepts are short plugs, so the drifting axial reading probably looked like
    a seat. This was not traced.
  - No controller re-tares the F/T sensor before contact.
- Post hoc, yaw and tilt: J, which is F's insertion plus C's VERIFY, seats 96-100% on unseen
  yaw and tilt, where F seats 37.5-91.2%. So most of C's lead there likely comes from C's
  VERIFY (its yaw re-centring), not from C's search.
- Post hoc, G: G and C give identical or nearly identical results in most cells, so G's
  learned prior gave nothing measurable here.

## Limits and open fairness items

- One seed and 40 trials per condition. A family cell needs a discordant split of about
  9 / 1 to reach p < 0.05, so a "matches" verdict (for example gantry Micro-Fit tolerance,
  6 / 1) is weak evidence of equality.
- Two of the bases are assumed: the F/T drift rate and the Micro-Fit tolerance.
- The unseen socket rotation turns the socket about its mouth centre, not about the board
  edge.
- The in-hand slip is fixed for the whole cycle.
- The tilt and yaw verdicts rest on the 3 deg score limits, fixed in V0.1. The cavity
  admits about 3.8 deg of tilt and 4.7 deg of yaw.
- Fairness: no fair baseline checks yaw or tilt after insertion. J shows what C's VERIFY
  adds to F. A conventional cell could add a vision or probe check of the seated part; that
  is not modelled.
- Fairness: none of A-J re-zeroes the F/T sensor just before contact. Doing so would likely
  remove most of the drift family's effect for every controller. This is an open item, not
  a fix to make in this round.
- heldout2 has now been used once, on seed 20261207. Any development against these families
  from now on makes them in-sample, as happened to `heldout`.
