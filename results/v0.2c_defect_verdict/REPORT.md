Development measurements in simulation. Branch `v0.2c/defect-verdict`, branched from 54f3097.

- **Code measured:** commit `afa08a4` for all `after/` runs and the gantry validation run. The arm validation ran at `f2ca43d`, whose code is identical. Later commits change only results.
- **Seeds:** 2026 and 2027 for development, 4002 for one pre-registered choice. No other seed was run.
- **Benchmark files:** I did not read any file under `results/benchmark_v0.2b`.
- **Tables:** `tables.md`, generated by `python -m experiments.v02c_tables` from the committed `before/` and `after/` runs.

## What changed

All new behaviour runs only when two conditions hold:
- the connector has a mating spec (the latching Micro-Fit);
- `AdaptiveConfig.defect_verdict` is on (the default).

On the housing-only connector none of it runs, and `defect_verdict=False` gives C as it was at Checkpoint 2. Every new constant is in `AdaptiveConfig` with a basis comment.

The evidence is observation-only: wrench, encoder depth and the controller's own touch calibration. It is compared with:
- the drawing: chamfer, clearance, mating depth;
- the datasheet: maximum mating force 32 N, minimum retention 58 N, terminal wipe 1.75 mm;
- the connector's force budget: stop at 48 N.

Nothing reads the simulator's defect parameters or defect labels.

1. **Hard-stop verdict, `defect_suspected:hard_stop_short`.**
   - **Candidate:** a stall (stall, bottomed or excess-force trigger) with the centre of pressure within 0.5 mm of the tip centre and little side load. That means the plug is supported under its whole tip. A face block next to the hole has an off-centre CoP.
   - **Inside the cavity, by depth:** the stop is past the lead-in by 3 depth sigmas, measured from the calibrated mouth.
   - **Inside the cavity, by capture probe:** used when the mouth was calibrated on that same stop, so the stop could be the face itself. The probe is the VERIFY wall-probe move; both walls must push back with 2 N within the clearance budget.
   - **Stops where the drawing puts the terminals** (seat minus wipe, less 0.5 mm) count only after a proof push. The stop must hold at 1.1 times the datasheet maximum mating force (35.2 N) without advancing 0.1 mm. That force is below the 40 N abort and the 48 N stop. If the plug advances, C keeps inserting at that force.
   - **Verdict:** two such stops at the same raw encoder depth (within 0.15 mm).
2. **No-retention verdict, `defect_suspected:no_retention`.**
   - When the plug slides out under the guarded pull, C retracts and retries as before, but first holds the next bottom at the proof force.
   - If the plug goes deeper, the first bottom was premature and the insertion continues.
   - Two slide-outs at the same bottom, each with the CoP under the plug face, give the verdict.
3. **Gentle retract.**
   - **Latched plug.** A plug that holds the pull-test force but showed no click is latched, or held some other way. The datasheet minimum retention (58 N) is above the cell stop (48 N), so the plug cannot be backed out without a stop. C re-seats it, declares `unverified:latched_no_click` and leaves it in place.
   - **Any retract on a latching part** stops pulling above 36 N (0.75 times the stop force). The first time this happens in a cycle, C re-seats the plug and runs the latch check there, because the plug may have latched while the attempt was cut short. After that, C declares `stuck_during_retract` and leaves the plug where it is.
4. **Polarization guard.** Once a hard stop has been seen inside the cavity, C does not start or continue the yaw search, and it keeps the yaw the plug entered with.
   - Reason: a tip past the lead-in is within the cavity's yaw capacity, so turning it only presses the plug into the walls.
   - This covers both the gantry force trips and the arm torque trips; both happened on attempts after a yaw-search step.
5. **Off by default:** a speed cap in the terminal zone, `latch_zone_speed_mm_s` (0 means off). See "What did not work or was left off".

The task asked for the verdict "after lateral and yaw search have been tried". The hard-stop verdict needs two stops, and C must have moved (localizer) or re-aimed (jam re-aim) between them. It does not wait for a yaw search: once a stop is known to be inside the cavity, yaw is not searched, for the reason in item 4.

- **Bad parts passed:** 0 of 960 after (two backends, two controllers, 240 each), the same as before. With 0 of 480 per backend, the Wilson upper limit on the escape rate is 0.8%, in this simulator.
- **Good-part success did not drop.** No good part that seated before failed after (0 lost in every cell), and 13 more seated after. None of the gains is significant on its own (p 0.06 to 0.5).
- **Time per bad part** fell from 21-26 s to 8 s on the gantry and to 12-13 s on the arm. Median per defect, gantry C:

- **Verdict coverage:** every gantry bad part ended with a verdict or a protective stop. On the arm, 2 of 240 per controller hit the 26 s time limit instead: one half-latched part and one wrong-polarization part.
- **Verdict labels:** the label does not always name the defect. The stubbed terminal is often bottomed as a seat (the depth estimate falls inside the seat window) and then called no_retention.
- **Polarization trips:** gantry wrong polarization, protective stops 1 to 0 (C) and 13 to 0 (G). Arm G, 1 to 0 (a 0.6 N m torque trip).
- **Retract trips:** stops while backing a latched plug out, in RECOVER after a failed latch check or in the FAILURE retract, went from 4 to 0. All four were on arm good parts before: C 1, G 3.
- **Good-part cycle time:** good parts that succeeded both before and after have identical cycle times, except on mf_noise_1mm. There, 1 to 3 of the 39 to 56 successes per cell are slower by 1.3 to 2.8 s, because of a capture probe on a repeated flat face block. That is +0.03 to +0.19 s per success on average.

## What got worse or is still open

- **False rejects on the arm: 5 of 180 (C) and 7 of 180 (G).** All are `unverified:latched_no_click`. Each part was latched and seated, but the click detector did not see the snap (the force drop was below 40% of the assumed 6 N latch peak).
  - Every one of these trials failed before too, by a 48 N stop while retracting the latched plug or by the time limit. So no good unit was lost relative to Checkpoint 2.
  - The cell now leaves a good, latched part in place, flagged for an operator, instead of tripping the stop.
  - The click detector misses on the arm and was not changed here. Seed 4002 shows the same pattern: 4 false rejects of 240 good-part trials, all latched without a click.
- **Half-latched first insertions still trip the stop on the gantry:** 12 of 60 trials for C and 11 of 60 for G, down from 20 and 19. They happen on the first insertion, before there is any evidence.
  - `stop_chatter/summary.json` (commit cf9e04d, seed 2026, trials 3 and 5) shows this end stop oscillating between 15 and 58 N at a frozen setpoint, with 0.1 mm amplitude. A good part at the floor holds 24.6 N steady.
  - The end stop is an explicit stiff force (5e5 N/m) applied at every physics step in `defex/sim/mating.py`. These stops are at least partly a property of that model, not of the controller. The simulator was not changed.
- **Remaining stops on arm good parts** (5 for C, 4 for G) are 0.6 N m torque trips during insertion or DETECT on mf_noise_1mm, as before. Not addressed.
- **Mislabelled good part in an earlier version.** In code at commit 7b540d5, never measured on the final runs, a good part wedged at 7.2 mm on the arm, with the CoP 5 mm off centre, slid out twice and was called no_retention.
  - The flat-bottom condition added in afa08a4 removed this. In the reported runs, no good part got a defect verdict.
  - A good part with a misread bottom could still get one; the dev data has no such case.
- **Capture probe risk:** the probe reuses VERIFY's thresholds. A yawed plug cradled on the 45 degree chamfer, at a friction higher than the suite's 0.3, could produce 2 N on both sides.
  - The probe only runs when the stop is the highest support seen and the camera puts it at least a lead-in below the mouth.
  - It has not misfired in these runs: when it ran on good parts, it reported "not captured" at 0.2 to 0.4 N.
- **Two arm bad parts per controller hit the time limit** without a verdict (see above).

## What did not work or was left off

- **Re-pushing a plug that slid out, from where the pull left it** (the first version). It released the setpoint lead stored at the terminal entry peak into the half-latch end stop and tripped the stop at 54 N. It was replaced by the proof push at the next bottom (item 2).
- **Terminal-zone speed cap** (`latch_zone_speed_mm_s`). At 5 mm/s it removed both first-insertion stops on the two half-latched dev trials checked by hand (gantry, seed 2026, trials 3 and 5). It also added 0.41 s to a good cycle (5.63 to 6.04 s, mf_nominal trial 0).
  - It was not measured on a full run, and it is left off.
  - Whether it is worth turning on depends on the defect rate and on whether the end-stop oscillation is real.

## Validation (seed 4002, pre-registered)

The rule was committed at f1ad138 (`validation/SELECTION_RULE.md`), before any run on 4002. The variants are in `validation/variants.json`; the data is in `validation/{gantry,arm}/trials.csv`.

Candidates:
- off: `defect_verdict` False, i.e. C as at Checkpoint 2;
- r2: `hard_stop_repeats` = `no_retention_repeats` = 2;
- r3: both set to 3.

Each candidate ran on C and on G, on both backends, 20 trials per condition.

There is a counting error in the rule file: it says 840 trials per candidate, but the true count is 560 (7 conditions × 20 trials × 2 backends × 2 controllers). The 240 good-part trials it states are correct. The rule was applied as written.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| off | 0 | 221/240 (88.0-94.9%) | reference | 0 | 3 / 20 | 23.85 | 80 / 70 |
| r2 | 0 | 223/240 (89.0-95.5%) | 0 / 2 (0.5) | 4 | 1 / 7 | 10.37 | 144 / 132 |
| r3 | 0 | 223/240 (89.0-95.5%) | 0 / 2 (0.5) | 4 | 1 / 12 | 14.67 | 115 / 104 |

Applying the rule:
1. No candidate passed a bad part.
2. Good-part success: all three are tied (McNemar p ≥ 0.05 against the best).
3. Good units per hour with 60 s per stop: r2 at 132, r3 at 104, off at 70.

**r2 is chosen; the defaults are unchanged.**

On this seed, as on the dev seeds, no good part got a defect verdict. All 4 false rejects under r2 are arm parts latched without a click.

## Tests

The full suite at 5054518 gives 112 passed.

New tests in `tests/test_defect_verdict.py`:
- the rules are off on housing-only connectors and with the switch;
- the proof force and the retract cap come from the datasheet and the budget;
- the hard-stop candidate rules;
- two identical in-cavity stops give the verdict, and stops at different depths do not;
- a stop at lead-in depth is not "in the cavity";
- the flat-bottom condition for slide-out evidence;
- a regression test for the latch-phase state;
- closed loop, gantry, dev seed 2026: wrong polarization and stubbed terminal get `hard_stop_short`, and broken latch gets `no_retention`, each without a stop and in under 12 s;
- a good part still seats with none of the new events;
- the housing-only suite logs none of the new events.
