# W5b: controller C on the UR5e arm (development)

Development measurements in simulation on dev seeds 2026 and 2027 only. Tables
are generated by `experiments/w5b_tables.py` into `tables.md`; every number
below comes from that file or the JSON files named next to it.

## Result

C now works on the UR5e for the standard set. It does not beat F on latching
parts under 1 mm vision noise.

| Arm, standard set (5 conditions) | C before | C after | D | F |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Seed 2026, 20 trials per cell: success | 39% | 86% | 83% | 84% |
| Seed 2026: protective stops | 44 | 3 | 2 | 0 |
| Seed 2026: failed units passed as good | 0 | 0 | 6 | 10 |
| Seed 2027, 10 trials per cell: success | 38% | 88% | 82% | 82% |
| Median cycle of successes, seed 2026 (s) | 11.39 | 8.41 | 6.52 | 6.51 |

| Arm, latching suite, good parts (mf_nominal / mf_moderate / mf_noise_1mm) | C before | C after | D | F |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Seed 2026, 20 trials | 19 / 18 / 3 | 20 / 20 / 13 | 12 / 14 / 5 | 20 / 20 / 17 |
| Seed 2027, 10 trials | 9 / 10 / 4 | 10 / 10 / 5 | 7 / 8 / 1 | 10 / 10 / 8 |

Bad parts passed as good, seeds 2026 and 2027 together: C 0 of 120 before and
after, D 51 of 120, F 85 of 120. C rejects bad parts by running to its time
limit (`aborted:time_limit` on 79 of 80 bad parts at seed 2026, the other a
retry limit), so each rejected bad part costs a full time-limit cycle.

The seed-2027 latching gain is small (23 to 25 of 30 good parts) and C's
protective stops there went from 0 to 3. Part of the seed-2026 latching gain
came from changes made after looking at seed-2026 failures (the lag-free stop
and the compliant latch pull) and did not carry over.

D, E and F: none of the new rules applies to them, and their rows are identical
before and after.

## Cause

The cause was servo following lag, not one of the three starting hypotheses.
Menagerie's UR5e joints are PD servos with kv/kp = 0.2 s, so the setpoint runs
about 0.2 s x speed ahead of the arm. When a guarded move meets a wall, that
lead is pushed into the wall.

- 43 of C's 44 protective stops on seed 2026 came in VERIFY's yaw sweep right
  after a passed wall probe: 0.69 deg of setpoint lead when the wall was met,
  1.8-2.3 N m within 40 ms against the 0.6 N m stop
  (`diagnosis/event_stats.json`, `diagnosis/traces_before.json`).
- W5's speed rule (stiffness x speed x 12 ms reaction) leaves out the 0.2 s
  term. A sweep inside the torque budget would need about 0.2 deg/s, too slow
  for the cycle limit.
- In 18 wall probes the 0.4 mm setpoint lead counted toward the probe's travel
  limit, so the probe gave up before reaching the far wall, and each retry
  repeated it until the time limit.

The three hypotheses:

- (a) Loop gains too high on the stiff wrist: true for the conform and
  rotational admittance loops (the seat conform rocked the plug between its
  edges, 8 torque sign changes in 0.8 s), but they did not cause the stops.
- (b) Centre-of-pressure model mismatch: true and largely fixed. In C's own
  top-face contacts the median CoP model error went from 3.95 mm (4% within
  0.5 mm, n = 102) to 0.25 mm (56%, n = 50); gantry 0.13 mm (68%, n = 34). The
  arm's 90th percentile is still 4.3 mm (`cop_model/*/summary.json`).
- (c) Face-contact forces: no problem. Median peak (10 ms) about 13 N, maximum
  20 N, against the 30 N stop and 45 N damage limit.

## What changed

Every rule is an exact no-op on the gantry: factors are exactly 1, added terms
exactly 0, new paths off.

- Two fixture properties measured by each backend: tilt stiffness (gantry
  15 N m/rad, UR5e 822 N m/rad) and servo following lag (gantry 0.037 s, UR5e
  0.20 s).
- Yaw rate limit counts lag beyond the gantry's.
- C's rotational and conform admittance damping is scaled by stiffness over
  15 N m/rad.
- The wall-probe travel limit is extended by speed x the extra lag.
- Where yaw cannot be swept at 5 deg/s or more, VERIFY checks yaw from the two
  wall contacts it already made: the free travel between the walls bounds |yaw|
  at 2 deg; the torque lever arm at a corner gives the direction for one turn
  and a second probe pair; implausible lever arms (a wedged plug) fail the
  check.
- On stiffer wrists, conform turns at a fixed rate (4 deg/s on the top face,
  1 deg/s on the cavity floor) toward the measured torque until it reverses or
  the tilt limit is reached.
- When a wall stops a turn or the conform, C commands the measured orientation
  so the stored lead is not pushed into the wall.
- On stiff wrists, the latch pull and re-seat keep the scaled lateral and
  rotational admittance active (without it the arm loaded the latched plug
  sideways, 6 N, and tripped the 0.6 N m stop).

New constants are in `AdaptiveConfig` with their basis, all set by hand on
seed 2026. The wall-yaw lever bound was widened from 1.5 to 2 half-lengths after
seeing seed-2026 corner contacts with 9-11 mm lever arms.

Later, in the Checkpoint 2 review (`results/checkpoint2_prep/review_fixes/`),
these rules were restricted to wrists at least 4x stiffer or servos at least 2x
slower than the gantry's, because the original thresholds also switched them on
inside the sim-randomization bands of the gantry.

## Gantry equivalence

Run on the untouched branch (fe80689, `equiv_before`) and on the final code
(28dc05d, `equiv_after`); `experiments/w5b_equivalence.py`, output in
`equivalence.txt`, compares every trials.csv field except wall_time_s:

- seed 2026, 20 trials, noise_1mm / noise_2mm / friction_1, C and F: 120
  trials, 0 differences;
- latching suite, seed 2026, 10 trials, C and F: 140 trials, 0 differences.

## Open issues

- The 0.2 s lag comes from Menagerie's actuators; a real UR5e under servoj may
  lag less. On hardware it must be identified from a logged ramp.
- noise_2mm on the arm is still weak: C 10/20 (2026) and 4/10 (2027). Failures
  are time limits (a failed verify retries the same probe), wedged partial
  insertions, and tilt errors beyond the conform's 4 deg range.
- Two of C's latching stops come from retracting a latched plug against the
  58 N latch after a failed click/pull verdict, which trips the 48 N stop.
- The wall-geometry yaw check assumes probes along the short axis of a
  rectangular cavity and cannot pass a plug yawed beyond 2 deg; tilt narrows
  free travel too, so it can reject good plugs (8 failed checks on seed 2026).
- D and F were not examined on the arm: when the spiral finds the hole the
  setpoint is about 0.4 mm ahead of the plug.

```
python -m experiments.robustness --backend arm --seed 2026 --trials 20 \
  --conditions nominal,lateral_3mm,noise_1mm,noise_2mm,friction_1 --controllers fixed,correction,adaptive,spiral,correction_rcc,spiral_rcc \
  --name w5b_development/after/arm_standard_seed2026
python -m experiments.robustness --backend arm --suite latching --seed 2026 --trials 20 \
  --name w5b_development/after/arm_latching_seed2026
python -m experiments.w5b_tables > results/w5b_development/tables.md
```
